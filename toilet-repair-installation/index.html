Why This Happens in Boulder.

Local Causes.

This comes up often in University Hill rental properties with heavy day-to-day use. Some of those early low-flow toilets were genuinely underpowered and clog more than modern low-flow designs, which have improved significantly. If you're fighting frequent clogs on an older low-flow unit, replacement with a modern model is often the more permanent fix rather than repeated repair.

Modern toilet fixtures also fall under water-usage standards set out in the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which Colorado has adopted statewide, and current low-flow models perform far better against those standards than the earliest low-flow generation did.

A running toilet is also worth fixing quickly for a practical reason: it wastes a genuinely significant amount of water over a month, often without the sound being loud enough to notice unless you happen to be in the bathroom when it cycles.

Know the Signs.

Common Warning Signs.

Water running continuously or intermittently without being flushed.

The toilet rocking slightly when you sit down (a wax ring or flange issue).

Frequent clogging even with normal use.

Water pooling around the base after flushing.

A weak flush that doesn't clear the bowl in one pass.

A tank that refills loudly or takes much longer than it used to.

How We Diagnose It.

Finding the Actual Problem.

A running toilet is almost always the flapper, fill valve, or flush valve, and we check all three rather than replacing parts by guesswork. A toilet that rocks usually means the wax ring has failed or the flange underneath is cracked or sitting too low, which is a different repair entirely.

For a weak flush, we check whether the issue is the flush valve, a partially clogged trap way, or simply an older low-flow design that was never particularly effective to begin with. Each of those has a different fix, and it's worth knowing which one you're dealing with before replacing anything.

Common Questions.

Straight Answers.

Why does my toilet clog so often even though nothing unusual goes down it?

Some early-generation low-flow toilets from the 1990s and early 2000s simply don't clear waste as well as current designs. If that matches your situation, replacement with a modern low-flow model usually solves it for good.

Is a rocking toilet a serious problem?

It's worth fixing promptly. A rocking toilet usually means the wax seal underneath is compromised, which can let water leak onto the subfloor over time and cause damage that's more expensive than the original repair.

Can you install a toilet I already purchased?

Yes, we're glad to install a unit you've already picked out, as long as it's compatible with your existing rough-in and drain configuration.

How much water does a running toilet actually waste?

More than most people expect, often enough to noticeably move a water bill over a full month. It's one of the more cost-effective small repairs we do for exactly that reason.
