Why This Happens in Boulder.

Why Boulder Homes See This.

This is a common kitchen call across newer Southeast Boulder condos and townhomes as much as older single-family homes. Leaks from underneath a disposal are almost always a failed seal, either where the disposal connects to the sink flange or where it connects to the drain line, rather than a problem with the motor itself. That distinction matters because a seal repair costs a fraction of a new unit.

Frequent jams are often a usage pattern issue rather than a defective unit. Fibrous foods like celery and corn husks, along with coffee grounds and grease, are common causes of jams and slow buildup even in a disposal that's otherwise working fine.

Know the Signs.

Warning Signs to Know.

A humming sound with no blade movement (usually a jam).

Water leaking from underneath the disposal.

The reset button tripping repeatedly.

Unusual grinding, rattling, or loud noise during use.

The disposal not turning on at all.

A persistent bad odor even after running the disposal with water.

How We Diagnose It.

How We Diagnose the Issue.

A humming disposal with no movement is almost always a jam we can clear safely, and it's worth checking before assuming the motor has failed. If the unit is leaking, we trace whether it's the sink flange seal, the drain connection, or the unit's internal seal, since each has a different fix.

A disposal that trips its reset button repeatedly is usually overheating from being overworked, often from grinding something it wasn't designed for, or from a motor genuinely nearing the end of its life. We check load and amperage to tell the two apart before recommending anything.

Common Questions.

Common Questions, Answered.

Is it safe to try to clear a disposal jam myself?

With the power disconnected at the switch or breaker, carefully checking for a visible obstruction is reasonable using the disposal's own wrench tool, not your fingers. Never reach into a disposal that's plugged in or that you haven't confirmed is powered off.

How long do garbage disposals typically last?

It varies widely with usage, but a unit that's failing repeatedly after several years of regular use is often past the point where repair makes more sense than replacement. We'll give you an honest read on which situation you're in.

What shouldn't go down a garbage disposal?

Fibrous vegetables like celery, coffee grounds in large amounts, grease or oil, and bones are the most common causes of jams and premature wear. None of these will necessarily break the unit immediately, but they add up over time.

Do you install disposals that connect to a dishwasher?

Yes, reconnecting the dishwasher drain line correctly is a standard part of any disposal replacement, and we test both the disposal and the dishwasher connection before we consider the job finished.
