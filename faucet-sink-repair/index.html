Why This Happens in Boulder.

The Boulder Factor.

We see this across the city, from older North Boulder fixtures to heavily-used University Hill rental units. Boulder's water hardness contributes to faucet cartridge and valve wear over time, since mineral buildup inside the cartridge is a common reason a faucet starts dripping or becomes hard to turn even when the fixture itself isn't old.

Older homes with original fixtures from a prior renovation sometimes have supply lines or shutoff valves that have never been touched since installation, and those can seize up or fail independently of the faucet itself.

Kitchen faucets see more daily use than any other fixture in the house, which is part of why they tend to be the first place cartridge wear shows up as an actual, noticeable drip rather than just a slightly stiff handle.

Know the Signs.

What to Look For.

A faucet that drips even when fully closed.

A handle or valve that's become hard to turn or feels loose.

Low water pressure at a specific faucet.

Water leaking from around the base of the faucet.

A sink that drains slowly even after clearing an obvious clog.

Discolored or mineral-crusted buildup around the faucet spout, handle, or aerator screen.

How We Diagnose It.

Getting to the Root Cause.

Most dripping faucets come down to a worn cartridge, O-ring, or valve seat, and we check which one before recommending anything. A faucet that's leaking at the base usually points to a different seal than one dripping from the spout.

We also check the condition of the shutoff valve under the sink while we're there, since a valve that's seized up or won't fully close is worth addressing at the same time rather than as a separate future call. Any faucet we install meets current water-usage standards adopted under Colorado's plumbing code.

Common Questions.

What Homeowners Ask.

Why does my faucet keep dripping even after I tighten it?

A drip usually means a worn internal part, most often a cartridge or O-ring, rather than anything you can fix by tightening the handle. That part needs to be replaced, not adjusted.

Can you install a faucet I bought myself?

Yes, as long as it's compatible with your existing sink configuration and supply lines. We're happy to take a look before you buy if you're unsure about compatibility, since not every faucet fits every sink or every existing hole pattern.

Why is the water pressure low at just one faucet?

That's usually a clogged aerator or a partially closed shutoff valve rather than a whole-house pressure problem. It's one of the quicker things we check and often resolves in minutes.

How long does a typical faucet replacement take?

Most single-faucet replacements are completed in under two hours, assuming the existing supply lines and shutoff valves are in reasonable condition and don't need separate repair first.
