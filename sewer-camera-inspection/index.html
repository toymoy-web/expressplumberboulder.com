Why This Happens in Boulder.

Why It Happens Here.

Older Boulder neighborhoods like Mapleton Hill and University Hill still have original clay sewer laterals in places. A camera inspection is often the only way to tell whether an aging line has root intrusion, offset joints, or just needs a routine cleaning.

Camera inspection is also increasingly common as part of pre-purchase due diligence for older Boulder homes, since a sewer line replacement is a significant cost that's worth knowing about before closing rather than after.

Know the Signs.

Signs to Watch For.

A clog that keeps recurring despite regular cabling.

Planning to buy an older home and wanting to check the sewer line first.

Multiple slow drains without an obvious single cause.

Preparing for a remodel that might affect existing drain lines.

Wanting documentation of pipe condition before a larger repair decision.

A previous plumber recommending replacement without showing you why.

How We Diagnose It.

How We Track Down the Cause.

The camera feeds a live video signal as it travels the line, letting us see cracks, root intrusion, bellies (low spots that trap water), offset joints, and material transitions in real time. We can also locate the exact position of a problem using the camera's built-in locator, which tells us precisely where to dig or access the pipe if repair is needed.

We walk you through what the camera finds in plain terms rather than handing you a technical report and moving on. If the line is in better shape than expected, we'll tell you that too instead of finding a reason to recommend work.

Common Questions.

Questions We Get Often.

Can a sewer camera inspection tell me if I need a full line replacement?

It's the best tool for making that call honestly. We can see the pipe's actual material, condition, and problem areas rather than recommending replacement based on age or guesswork alone.

How long does a camera inspection take?

Most residential inspections take under an hour, depending on line length and how many access points are available.

Can I get a copy of the video for my records?

Yes, that's standard, especially useful if you're using the inspection for a real estate transaction or to document a problem for a future repair decision.

Do I need to be home for a camera inspection?

Not necessarily, as long as we have access to a cleanout or another entry point into the line. We're happy to walk you through what we found by phone or video afterward either way.

Can a camera inspection find a sewer line easement or property line issue?

Locating equipment can identify where the line runs, which sometimes surfaces exactly this kind of question, though it's worth confirming property boundary details with your county records directly.

If the inspection reveals a section that needs fixing, sewer line repair covers the actual repair methods. A line that's clogged rather than damaged is a simpler drain cleaning job, and heavy buildup along the pipe wall is often best cleared with hydro jetting before a follow-up inspection.
