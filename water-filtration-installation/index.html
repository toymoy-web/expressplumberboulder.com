Why This Happens in Boulder.

Local Causes.

This comes up as often in newer Gunbarrel and Southeast Boulder construction as it does in older homes. Boulder's municipal water generally meets safety standards, but taste, chlorine smell, and sediment are common reasons homeowners look into filtration even when the water is technically safe. Homes on private wells within unincorporated parts of Boulder County have a different set of considerations entirely and typically need a water test before choosing a system.

Boulder's annual water quality report is public information, and it's worth a look before assuming what's actually in your water. Filtration decisions are more effective when they're based on your specific water rather than a general assumption about municipal supplies.

Know the Signs.

Common Warning Signs.

A noticeable chlorine taste or smell from tap water.

Visible sediment or cloudiness in water.

Wanting to address a specific contaminant found in water testing.

Preferring filtered water over bottled water for cost or convenience reasons.

A private well with no existing treatment system.

Buying an older home with unknown or outdated existing filtration.

How We Diagnose It.

Finding the Actual Problem.

We start with what you've actually noticed, then test water quality to confirm or rule out specific concerns before recommending a system. A whole-home system addresses water at every tap, while a point-of-use system under the kitchen sink addresses drinking and cooking water specifically at lower cost.

For well water specifically, we test for a broader set of concerns than municipal water typically requires, since well water quality can vary meaningfully even between neighboring properties.

Common Questions.

Straight Answers.

Do I need filtration if my water already passes safety standards?

Not necessarily for safety, but taste, smell, and sediment are legitimate reasons to consider it even when the water is technically safe. It comes down to your own preferences.

What's the difference between filtration and softening?

Softening addresses hardness minerals specifically. Filtration addresses taste, chlorine, sediment, and specific contaminants. Many homes benefit from both, and they can be installed together.

How often do filtration system filters need to be replaced?

It depends on the system and your water usage, but we'll walk you through the specific maintenance schedule for whatever system you choose rather than leaving you to guess.

Can filtration remove hard water minerals too?

Standard filtration isn't designed for that. Hardness minerals are addressed by a water softener specifically, which is why we sometimes recommend pairing both systems together in the same installation visit.
