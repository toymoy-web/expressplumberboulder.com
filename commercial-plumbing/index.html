Why This Happens in Boulder.

Local Causes.

Boulder's mix of older downtown commercial buildings and newer build-outs in business parks near the Diagonal Highway means commercial plumbing calls range widely, from a decades-old cast iron stack in a University Hill storefront to a modern build-out with entirely different fixture standards.

Restaurants and food service properties see a specific recurring issue: grease trap backups that seem sudden but are actually the result of gradual buildup finally reaching a tipping point. Regular maintenance catches this before it becomes an emergency closure.

Know the Signs.

Common Warning Signs.

A grease trap backing up or overflowing.

Multiple fixtures on the same line slowing down at once.

A leak in a shared wall or ceiling affecting more than one tenant space.

Water pressure issues affecting an entire floor or suite.

Backflow concerns tied to a commercial kitchen or irrigation system.

Aging fixtures or piping in an older downtown building nearing end of service life.

How We Diagnose It.

Finding the Actual Problem.

For commercial leak calls, we often start with a video camera inspection since commercial buildings tend to have longer, more complex line runs than a residential property. That's a faster and less disruptive way to find the actual source than opening walls speculatively.

We also work around your operating hours where possible, since a restaurant or retail space often can't afford unplanned downtime during business hours for anything short of a genuine emergency. Before we arrive for an active leak, shutting off the water at the property's main valve, if a staff member knows its location, limits damage in the meantime.

For multi-tenant buildings, we also check whether an issue is isolated to one suite or affects shared plumbing infrastructure serving multiple tenants, since that changes who needs to be looped in before work starts.

Common Questions.

Straight Answers.

Do you work with property management companies?

Yes, we regularly coordinate directly with property managers on multi-tenant buildings, and we're glad to set up a standing maintenance schedule rather than handling everything as one-off emergency calls.

Can you service a restaurant's grease trap on a recurring schedule?

Yes, and it's genuinely worth doing on a schedule rather than waiting for a backup. A trap serviced regularly rarely turns into an emergency closure.

Do you handle backflow testing for commercial kitchens?

Yes, backflow prevention is often a code requirement for commercial food service and irrigation connections, and we handle both testing and repair.

Can you respond after hours for a commercial emergency?

Yes, a genuine emergency like an active leak or complete loss of water service gets an urgent response regardless of the hour, since a closed business often can't wait until normal service hours.

Do you provide invoices suitable for business accounting?

Yes, we provide detailed invoices with the documentation most businesses need for their own records, expense reporting, or property management accounting systems.
