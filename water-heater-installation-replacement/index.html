Why This Happens in Boulder.

Why Boulder Homes See This.

This comes up often in South Boulder homes from the original 1960s construction wave now reaching replacement age. Replacement is also the natural point to fix an existing venting problem rather than repeat it. A unit installed years ago into a shared masonry chimney or an undersized flue can be corrected during replacement instead of carried forward into the new unit.

Households considering tankless for the first time often ask about the upfront cost difference. Tankless units cost more to install but less to run over time, and they take up a fraction of the space a tank does, which matters in some of Boulder's smaller older homes.

Know the Signs.

Warning Signs to Know.

The existing tank is past 10 to 12 years old.

Repeated repairs on the same unit within the past year or two.

Rusty water or visible corrosion on the tank itself.

Wanting to switch from tank to tankless for space or efficiency.

A unit that's undersized for current household hot water demand.

Planning a bathroom or kitchen remodel that touches the water heater's location.

How We Diagnose It.

How We Diagnose the Issue.

Before recommending a specific unit, we look at household size, peak simultaneous hot water demand, and the existing gas line or electrical circuit capacity. A unit sized for a smaller household than yours will run out of hot water regardless of how new it is.

We also check the condition of the shutoff valves, drip leg, and expansion tank while we're there. These small components are easy to overlook during a straight swap, but a failing one undermines the reliability of an otherwise brand-new water heater.

A full replacement also has to meet the current International Plumbing Code (IPC) standards Colorado has adopted, which is why an expansion tank or updated drip leg sometimes gets added even on a like-for-like swap.

Your Options.

What We Recommend.

Tank Replacement.

  • Lower upfront cost than tankless.
  • Simpler installation, especially as a like-for-like swap.
  • Typical service life of 10 to 15 years.

Tankless Conversion.

  • Endless hot water, no standby heat loss.
  • Compact wall-mounted unit frees up floor space.
  • Higher upfront cost, lower operating cost over the unit's life.

Common Questions.

Common Questions, Answered.

How long does a water heater installation take?

A like-for-like tank replacement is usually a same-day job. A tank-to-tankless conversion or a job that needs venting changes can take longer, and we'll tell you which situation you're in before scheduling.

Do you install all major brands?

Yes. We can also help you weigh brand and capacity options based on your household's actual usage rather than just matching what's currently installed.

What happens to my old water heater?

We haul it away and dispose of it properly as part of the installation, so you don't need to arrange separate removal.

Should I upgrade to a larger tank when replacing?

Only if your current tank is actually undersized for how the household uses hot water. We'll ask about peak usage times, like back-to-back showers or running the dishwasher and laundry together, before recommending a bigger unit than you need.

Does a new water heater need a permit in Boulder?

Most replacements do require a permit and inspection under Colorado's adopted plumbing code, and we handle that process as part of the installation rather than leaving it for you to arrange separately.
