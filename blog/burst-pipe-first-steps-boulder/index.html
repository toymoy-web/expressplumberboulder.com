What to Do Before the Plumber Arrives for a Burst Pipe

By the Boulder Express Plumbing team.

Talk to a Plumber.
(303) 552-3896
Call Now

Why Speed Actually Matters Here.

Most plumbing problems can wait a few hours without getting meaningfully worse. A burst pipe is the exception. Water is actively escaping the system, and every minute it continues adds to the eventual cleanup and repair cost. That's why the first few steps you take, before a technician even arrives, genuinely change the outcome.

Step One: Find the Main Shutoff.

Every home has a main water shutoff valve, and knowing where it is before you need it is worth five minutes of your time right now, not during an emergency. It's typically located where the water line enters the house, often in a basement, crawlspace, or utility closet near the water heater. Turning it fully clockwise stops water from feeding the leak while you wait for help.

If you genuinely don't know where yours is, call us. We can talk you through finding it over the phone, which is faster than searching room to room while water is actively running.

Step Two: Address Electrical Risk Before Anything Else.

If water is pooling anywhere near outlets, a electrical panel, or plugged-in appliances, don't wade into it to unplug something. Shut off power to that specific area at the breaker instead. This is a real safety priority, not an overcautious suggestion, and it should happen before you worry about protecting belongings.

Step Three: Limit the Damage, Don't Try to Fix It.

Once the water is shut off and the electrical risk is addressed, moving furniture and valuables away from the affected area helps, and photographing the damage before you move anything is genuinely useful if you end up filing an insurance claim. What you shouldn't do is attempt to repair the pipe yourself or assume a quick tape-and-hope fix will hold. Our burst pipe repair page covers what an actual repair involves, including checking whether the surrounding pipe section shows signs of the same age-related or freeze-related failure that caused the initial break.

When Water Damage Becomes Its Own Problem.

If water ran for any length of time before you caught it, or if it reached drywall, flooring, or insulation, that's a separate concern from the pipe repair itself. Our water damage restoration page covers the extraction and drying process, which genuinely needs to start within the first day or two to keep mold risk under control. Homes in older parts of the city, including Downtown Boulder and University Hill, where dense, heavily-used properties sometimes have less accessible plumbing, can see water travel further than expected before anyone notices.

What Happens After the Repair.

Once the immediate break is fixed and the water's back on, it's worth asking a broader question: was this an isolated failure, or a sign the surrounding pipe section is also nearing the end of its useful life. A technician who only patches the visible break without checking nearby pipe condition is setting you up for the same call again in a few months.

For older galvanized supply lines especially, a burst is sometimes the first visible sign of broader corrosion that's been developing for years. If that's the case, it's worth having an honest conversation about whether a targeted repair or a broader section replacement makes more sense for your specific situation, rather than just patching the same spot repeatedly.

Keep a Few Things on Hand.

A handful of simple preparations, all free and worth doing today rather than during an emergency, make a real difference the next time a burst pipe happens. Know the exact location of your main shutoff valve and make sure it actually turns freely, since a valve that has never been touched in years can seize up exactly when you need it most. Keep our number saved in your phone, and consider labeling the shutoff valve itself so anyone in the household can find it quickly during a stressful moment. If anyone else lives in or regularly visits your home, make sure they know where it is too, not just you. A house sitter or family member who can act quickly in your absence is worth more than any amount of preparation you do alone, and it's worth a five-minute walkthrough with them rather than assuming they'll figure it out under pressure.

Common Questions.

Should I turn the water heater off too during a burst pipe emergency?

If the burst pipe is on the cold water line feeding the house, shutting off the main valve also stops water from reaching the heater, so a separate step usually isn't necessary. If you're unsure which line is affected, mentioning that when you call helps us tell you exactly what to do.

Is it normal for water pressure to drop suddenly if a pipe bursts?

Yes, a sudden, dramatic drop in pressure throughout the house is a common sign of a significant break somewhere in the system, even before you've located the actual leak.

How much water damage can happen before a plumber arrives?

It depends heavily on how quickly the main valve gets shut off. A pipe left running for even 20 to 30 minutes can soak a significant area, which is exactly why finding and closing that valve immediately matters more than almost anything else you can do.

Do you offer any way to test my shutoff valve before an actual emergency?

Yes, turning the main valve fully off and back on during a routine visit confirms it moves freely and seals properly, which is worth doing periodically rather than discovering a seized valve during an actual emergency.
