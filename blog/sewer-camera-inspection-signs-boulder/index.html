Signs Your Sewer Line Needs a Camera Inspection

By the Boulder Express Plumbing team.

Talk to a Plumber.
(303) 552-3896
Call Now

Why Guessing at Sewer Problems Costs More.

A sewer line problem is one of the few plumbing issues where guessing at the cause, rather than actually seeing it, tends to be genuinely expensive. Recommending a repair method based on symptoms alone risks either underdoing it, cabling a line that actually needs jetting or structural repair, or overdoing it, replacing a line that only needed a targeted fix. A camera inspection removes the guesswork entirely by showing the pipe's actual, current condition directly on screen as the camera travels.

The Clearest Sign: A Clog That Keeps Coming Back.

If cabling a drain clears it temporarily, only for the same clog to return within weeks, that's the single strongest signal a camera inspection is worth doing before another round of cabling. A recurring clog in the same location usually points to something structural, root intrusion or a damaged section, that a cable clears through but doesn't actually fix.

Buying an Older Boulder Home.

If you're purchasing a home built before the 1960s, particularly in North Boulder or areas with original clay sewer laterals, a camera inspection during due diligence tells you the line's actual condition before you own the problem. A sewer line replacement is a significant cost, and knowing about it before closing gives you real negotiating leverage or at least accurate budgeting for what's ahead.

Planning a Remodel That Touches Drain Lines.

Any renovation that involves moving or extending drain lines is a natural point to confirm the existing line's condition first, since connecting new work to a compromised old line just moves the problem downstream rather than solving it. This is a smaller version of the same logic that applies to a full pre-renovation plumbing check.

What You Get From the Inspection Itself.

A camera inspection shows cracks, root intrusion, bellies where the line has settled and trapped water, and any offset joints, in real time as the camera travels the line. We also mark the exact position of anything significant using the camera's built-in locator, and provide video documentation you can keep, whether that's for your own records, a real estate transaction, or as the basis for a sewer line repair decision.

What Happens After the Inspection.

If the line looks genuinely healthy, that's a useful, reassuring outcome in its own right, and it means you've avoided an unnecessary repair based on a false assumption. That certainty alone is often worth the entire cost of the inspection for a homeowner who's been living with genuine, nagging uncertainty about the true, actual, current condition of their own aging sewer line. Many homeowners go months or even years without ever actually knowing for certain one way or the other, simply living with the nagging, unresolved doubt sitting in the back of their mind the whole time. If the inspection finds an issue, we walk through the actual footage with you rather than just handing over a technical summary, so you understand exactly what was found and why we're recommending a specific fix rather than a different one.

For issues that don't need immediate attention but are worth monitoring, like early-stage root intrusion that hasn't yet caused a blockage, we'll tell you that too, along with a realistic sense of the timeline before it's likely to become a bigger problem. We'd rather give you an accurate picture than manufacture urgency around something that can genuinely wait.

How Long the Footage Stays Useful.

A camera inspection is a snapshot of the line's condition at that specific moment, and conditions do change over time, particularly with active root intrusion. For a property you're not planning to sell or renovate soon, that snapshot is still useful for years as a baseline. For a real estate transaction specifically, a recent inspection carries considerably more weight than one done years earlier, since a lot can change in an older line over that span of time.

Common Questions.

How much does a sewer camera inspection typically cost compared to just cabling again?

It's a modest additional cost compared to another round of cabling, and it can save significantly more than that by avoiding an unnecessary repeat cabling on a line that actually needs a different fix.

Will the inspection tell me for certain whether I need a full sewer line replacement?

In most cases, yes. Seeing the pipe's actual condition, material, and the extent of any damage is the most reliable way to make that call, rather than guessing based on the home's age or a single symptom.

How often should a sewer line be camera-inspected as routine maintenance?

For most homes, there's no fixed schedule, it's driven by symptoms or a specific event like a home purchase or renovation rather than a calendar. An older home with a known history of issues might warrant a check every few years as a precaution.

Can a camera inspection damage my pipes?

No. The camera itself is a non-invasive tool that travels through the existing line without cutting or altering anything, which is part of why it's the preferred first step before any invasive work.

How far down the line can a camera actually reach?

Modern inspection cameras can typically travel well beyond a standard residential lateral's length, so reach isn't usually the limiting factor. Sharp bends or significant blockages are more likely to limit how far the camera can go than distance alone, and we'll tell you plainly and honestly if either one becomes a real constraint during your specific line's inspection.
