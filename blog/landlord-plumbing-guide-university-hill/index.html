A Landlord's Guide to Plumbing Near CU Boulder

By the Boulder Express Plumbing team.

Talk to a Plumber.
(303) 552-3896
Call Now

Rental Turnover Changes the Math.

A property in University Hill that's housed rotating student tenants for years puts more cumulative wear on its plumbing than a comparable owner-occupied home sees over the same period. It's not that student tenants are harder on a property intentionally, it's simply a volume-of-use factor: more people cycling through the same kitchen and bathroom fixtures. That turnover often comes without the same long-term investment in reporting small issues early, since tenants know they'll likely move again within a year or two regardless.

What Landlords Call About Most.

Drain clogs are the single most common call we get from rental properties in this part of Boulder, and original cast iron drain lines common in older University Hill properties weren't designed for the turnover a modern student household puts through them. Our drain cleaning page covers both routine cabling and, for lines with recurring buildup, hydro jetting as a more thorough option.

Beyond drains, general wear on fixtures, faucets, toilets, garbage disposals, shows up more frequently in high-turnover rentals simply due to usage volume. Our general plumbing repair page covers the kind of catch-all fixture and system issues that come up most in this setting.

Coordinating Around Lease Turnover.

One practical advantage of managing rental plumbing is that lease turnover dates are predictable and create a natural window for repairs that are easier to do in an empty unit. We're used to scheduling around these turnover periods for landlords who aren't on-site day to day, and we can provide documentation of work completed for your own property records or to address a tenant's move-out concerns.

A Proactive Approach Saves Money Over Time.

Reactive plumbing management, waiting for a tenant to report a problem, tends to cost more over the life of a rental property than a periodic proactive check. A brief inspection between tenants, especially for older cast iron drain lines or original fixtures, catches developing issues before they become an emergency call during someone's tenancy.

What Tenants Actually Report, and What They Don't.

Student tenants, understandably focused on classes and a busy schedule, don't always report a slow drain or a minor drip the moment it starts the way a homeowner living in the property long-term might. A small issue can go unreported for weeks, which gives it time to develop into something bigger before anyone calls. Making it genuinely simple and easy for a tenant to report an issue, a clear number to call and a landlord who responds quickly, measurably reduces how long small problems sit before they're addressed. That responsiveness also tends to improve how tenants treat the property more broadly over the course of a lease.

Multi-Unit Properties Add Another Layer.

If you own a multi-unit property in University Hill, shared plumbing infrastructure between units means an issue in one unit can sometimes affect another, particularly with older shared drain stacks common in converted historic homes. Understanding which parts of the system are shared versus unit-specific helps you communicate accurately with tenants about whose responsibility a given repair falls under, and helps us diagnose the actual source faster when something goes wrong.

Documentation That Actually Helps at Move-Out.

Detailed invoices and service records aren't just useful for your own bookkeeping. They also give you a documented, defensible position if a security deposit dispute ever comes down to whether damage was pre-existing or tenant-caused. Keeping a running record of plumbing service across each unit, rather than relying on memory, makes those conversations considerably easier when they come up. It also helps establish a maintenance history if you ever sell the property.

Setting Expectations With New Tenants.

A short written note to new tenants at move-in helps considerably. Covering basics like where the water shutoff is and what counts as an emergency worth calling about immediately, versus something that can wait for business hours, genuinely reduces both unnecessary emergency calls and delayed reports of real problems. Most tenants have never lived somewhere long enough to learn this on their own, and a little guidance upfront saves confusion later.

Budgeting for Plumbing as a Landlord.

Rental properties in high-turnover markets like University Hill benefit from treating plumbing maintenance as a predictable annual line item rather than an unplanned expense that shows up whenever something breaks. Setting aside a reasonable maintenance budget each year, informed by the property's age and actual service history, makes it easier to say yes to a proactive fix rather than deferring it until it becomes an emergency.

Common Questions.

Can you bill directly to a property management company instead of the tenant?

Yes, we regularly work directly with property managers and landlords rather than requiring tenants to coordinate or pay for repairs themselves.

How often should rental property plumbing be inspected?

Between tenants is a natural, low-disruption checkpoint for a general walkthrough, especially for older properties. Beyond that, responding promptly to any tenant-reported issue prevents most small problems from becoming bigger ones.

What's the most common plumbing complaint you get from University Hill landlords?

Recurring drain clogs, by a wide margin, usually tied to original cast iron lines carrying more volume than they were designed for. It's also the issue most likely to resolve permanently with the right diagnostic approach rather than repeated cabling.

Should I budget differently for plumbing on a property with more than four tenants?

It's reasonable to assume higher usage volume translates to more frequent service needs, particularly for drains and fixtures. We can help you build a realistic maintenance budget based on your specific property's size and history.
