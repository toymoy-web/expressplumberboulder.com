Water Damage 101: What Happens in the First 24 Hours

By the Boulder Express Plumbing team.

Talk to a Plumber.
(303) 552-3896
Call Now

Why the First Day Matters More Than Any Other.

Water damage is one of the few home emergencies where the timeline itself is a real factor in the outcome, not just the initial cause. Conditions favorable to mold growth can develop within 24 to 48 hours of materials staying wet, which means the response speed in that first day genuinely affects whether flooring, drywall, and belongings can be saved or need to be replaced.

Hour One: Stop the Source.

Before anything else, the water source needs to actually be stopped, whether that's a burst pipe, a failed appliance connection, or a failed sump pump letting groundwater in. This is what water mitigation covers specifically, the immediate response to confirm the source is stopped and begin containing the damage before it spreads further into unaffected areas.

The First Few Hours: Extraction Begins.

Once the source is confirmed stopped, extracting standing water starts immediately using pumps and extraction equipment. The longer water sits, the further it migrates into subfloor, walls, and insulation beyond what's visible on the surface, which is why this step doesn't wait for a scheduled appointment the way a routine repair would.

The First Day: Mapping and Drying.

Moisture meters are used to find water that's migrated beyond the visible wet area, since water damage often extends further than it appears. That mapping determines what actually needs industrial drying equipment versus what's genuinely unaffected. Our water damage restoration page covers this fuller process, including the air movers and dehumidifiers deployed to dry the structure once extraction is complete.

Documentation Along the Way.

Throughout this process, documenting the affected areas and likely cause is genuinely useful for any insurance claim, separate from the drying work itself. Photos taken before anything is moved, combined with a clear record of when the damage was discovered and addressed, give you the strongest possible position if you need to file a claim afterward. Insurance adjusters work from documentation, not memory, so the more you capture in the moment the better, even details that seem obvious to you in the moment may not be obvious weeks later when the claim is actually reviewed.

Beyond the First Day.

Extraction and initial drying are just the start. Equipment often needs to run for several days to fully dry affected materials, and moisture levels are monitored throughout that period rather than assumed to be resolved once the visible water is gone. Materials that can be dried in place are dried rather than automatically removed, since unnecessary demolition adds cost without improving the outcome when the underlying material is actually salvageable.

Once drying is confirmed complete, any structural restoration, replacing drywall or flooring that couldn't be saved, happens as a coordinated next phase rather than a separate, disconnected project handled by a different crew with no context on what came before. Treating the whole process as one continuous response, rather than a series of separately scheduled appointments, is what actually keeps a bad situation from getting worse while everyone waits between steps.

Why Coordination With the Plumbing Repair Matters.

If the water damage was caused by a plumbing failure, a burst pipe or a failed connection, that repair needs to happen alongside the restoration work, not as an afterthought once drying is complete. Starting extraction before the source is actually fixed just means treating the symptom while the underlying cause continues. We coordinate both pieces together so you're not left managing two separate vendors on two separate timelines during an already stressful situation.

What This Means for Your Insurance Claim.

Most homeowner's policies distinguish between sudden, accidental water damage and damage from a slow, ongoing issue that went unaddressed, and that distinction can affect coverage. Acting quickly once damage is discovered, rather than waiting a few days to see if it's really necessary, works in your favor both for limiting the actual damage and for demonstrating a reasonable response if your claim is ever reviewed.

When to Call Immediately vs. When You Have a Little Time.

Active water intrusion, a burst pipe, a failed sump pump during a storm, or standing water actively spreading, needs an immediate call, not a next-business-day appointment. A smaller, already-contained issue, like a slow leak that's been dripping into a bucket, still deserves prompt attention but has a bit more flexibility in timing. Knowing which situation you're actually in helps you communicate the right urgency when you call.

Common Questions.

Is it really true that mold can start within 24 to 48 hours?

Conditions favorable to mold growth can develop in that window when materials stay wet, which is why extraction and drying start immediately rather than waiting. It doesn't mean mold is guaranteed at exactly that mark, but it's a real enough risk that speed matters.

Can I start cleaning up water damage myself before help arrives?

If it's safe to do so, moving valuables away from the area and avoiding electrical equipment near standing water are both reasonable. Removing materials like drywall yourself isn't recommended, since it can complicate both the drying process and any insurance documentation.

How do I know if my belongings can actually be saved?

It depends heavily on the material and how long it was wet. Porous materials like drywall and carpet padding are the least likely to be salvageable, while many hard, non-porous items dry out fine with prompt attention.

Does every water damage situation require professional drying equipment?

For anything beyond a very small, quickly caught spill, yes. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry materials faster and more thoroughly than passive air drying, which matters directly for whether mold conditions develop in that critical first day or two.
