Why This Happens in Boulder.

The Boulder Factor.

Tankless units installed in Boulder also need venting sized correctly for the altitude, the same consideration that applies to tank-style water heaters. An improperly vented unit can trip a safety shutoff code even when the heat exchanger itself is fine.

Know the Signs.

What to Look For.

An error code displayed on the unit's control panel.

Water temperature that fluctuates between hot and lukewarm.

Reduced flow rate compared to when the unit was new.

A noticeable delay before hot water arrives at the tap.

The unit shutting off mid-use, especially with multiple fixtures running.

What's Actually Going On.

It's Throwing a Code and I Don't Know What It Means.

Most tankless calls start here: the unit's display is showing an error code, or it's simply not heating the way it used to, and the owner isn't sure whether that means a repair, a service, or a replacement. In our experience, a large share of these calls turn out to be scale buildup or a venting issue rather than a failed unit.

Boulder's water hardness is enough that tankless units genuinely need periodic descaling to keep their heat exchanger working efficiently. A unit that's never been serviced since installation is the single most common reason we see reduced output or an error code on an otherwise healthy unit.

How We Diagnose It.

Getting to the Root Cause.

We start by reading the specific error code, since each manufacturer's codes point to a fairly narrow set of causes. From there we check venting, water flow sensors, and scale buildup in the heat exchanger before assuming any part needs replacing.

A flush and descale resolves a surprising number of these calls on its own. Hard water minerals coat the heat exchanger over time, and clearing that buildup restores both temperature consistency and flow rate without touching the unit's electronics.

Your Options.

Fixing It the Right Way.

Flush & Descale.

  • Standard service for reduced output or minor error codes.
  • Restores heat exchanger efficiency.
  • Recommended periodically, not just when there's a problem.

Component Repair.

  • Water flow sensor, igniter, or control board replacement.
  • Needed when descaling doesn't resolve the code.
  • Usually same-visit once the part is identified.

Common Questions.

What Homeowners Ask.

How often does a tankless water heater need to be descaled?

It depends on your water hardness and usage, but going several years with no service at all is the most common reason we see reduced performance. If you don't know when yours was last serviced, that's worth checking.

Can you fix a tankless unit, or do I need a new one?

Most error codes are fixable with a flush, descale, or a single component repair. Full replacement usually only makes sense when the heat exchanger itself has failed, which is not the most common outcome.

Why does my tankless unit run cold when two showers run at once?

That's usually a flow rate or sizing issue rather than a failure. Some units simply weren't sized for simultaneous high-demand use in the home they're installed in, and that's worth discussing before assuming something is broken.
