Why This Happens in Boulder.

Why Boulder Homes See This.

Boulder sits on claystone and bentonitic clay soils that expand when wet and contract when dry, a pattern we see everywhere from South Boulder's 1960s slab homes to newer construction. That constant, slow movement puts real stress on slab plumbing over years, which is a genuine reason Boulder sees more slab leaks than cities on more stable soil, not a sales pitch. Repairs to water distribution piping, slab or otherwise, follow the same International Plumbing Code standards Colorado has adopted statewide.

Know the Signs.

Warning Signs to Know.

A warm spot on an otherwise cool floor (hot water line leak).

The sound of water running when every fixture is off.

Unexplained damp carpet or warped flooring in one area.

A water bill spike with no other explanation.

Low water pressure in one part of the house only.

What's Actually Going On.

Water Coming Up Under the Floor Near the Foundation.

This exact pattern, water or dampness appearing near where the garage meets the house foundation, or under floorboards with no obvious source, is one of the clearest slab-leak signals we see. It usually means a hot or cold water line running under the slab has developed a pinhole leak, and water is finding the path of least resistance up through the floor.

How We Diagnose It.

How We Diagnose the Issue.

We use electronic leak-tracing equipment and acoustic listening tools to pinpoint the leak's exact location before recommending any concrete work. In most cases we can narrow the leak to a specific few feet of slab, which keeps the repair itself small and targeted.

We also check the water heater's location relative to the leak, since a slab leak near a garage or utility closet is often the hot water line running from the tank, not the cold supply. That distinction changes both the likely leak path and the repair approach.

Your Options.

What We Recommend.

Spot Repair (Access Through Slab).

  • Once pinpointed, we cut only the section of concrete needed.
  • Fastest option when it's a single, isolated leak.
  • Best fit when the rest of the line is in good condition.

Re-Route Above Slab.

  • Reroutes the water line through a wall or ceiling instead of repairing under the slab.
  • Avoids cutting concrete entirely.
  • Often the better option in an older home with more than one weak point in the under-slab line.

Common Questions.

Common Questions, Answered.

How do you find a slab leak without tearing up the whole floor?

Electronic leak-tracing equipment picks up the sound signature of water escaping under pressure, which lets us narrow the leak's location down to a small, specific area before any concrete is touched at all.

Does homeowner's insurance cover slab leak repair?

Coverage varies by policy, and we're not in a position to promise what your specific policy covers. Many policies cover sudden, accidental water damage but not the underlying pipe repair itself. Check with your insurer, and we're glad to provide documentation of what we found for a claim, including the location and cause we identified during diagnosis.
