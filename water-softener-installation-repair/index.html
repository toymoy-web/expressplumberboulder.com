Why This Happens in Boulder.

Why It Happens Here.

Front Range water hardness varies somewhat by neighborhood and source, but Boulder's water is hard enough overall that softener systems earn their keep here, particularly for extending the life of water heaters and reducing scale buildup in tankless units and fixtures.

Homes on the city water system and homes on a private well within Boulder County can see meaningfully different hardness levels, so we test rather than assume based on neighborhood alone.

Any system we install meets recognized water treatment performance standards for softener capacity and salt efficiency, rather than an off-brand unit with no real testing behind its claims.

Know the Signs.

Signs to Watch For.

Soap that doesn't lather well, or leaves a film on skin and dishes.

Water spots and scale buildup on fixtures and glassware.

Salt in the brine tank not depleting normally.

A noticeable change in water taste or feel.

Scale buildup inside a water heater or tankless unit.

Dry skin or dull-feeling hair after showering, even with the same products.

What's Actually Going On.

The System Isn't Working Right and I Want It Checked.

This is the most common way a softener call starts: something feels off, maybe the water doesn't feel as soft as it used to, or salt usage has changed, but the homeowner isn't sure what's actually wrong. Before quoting anything, we check the resin bed, the brine tank, and the control valve, since any one of the three can cause the same general symptom.

How We Diagnose It.

How We Track Down the Cause.

We test water hardness before and after the softener to confirm it's actually regenerating and treating water correctly. A unit that looks like it's running but isn't cycling through regeneration properly will still let hard water through even with salt in the tank.

If you're installing for the first time, we test your actual water hardness rather than sizing off a regional average, since hardness can vary meaningfully even within Boulder depending on the source.

Common Questions.

Questions We Get Often.

How do I know if my water softener is actually working?

A simple hardness test on water from a treated tap tells us quickly. If the water still tests hard even with the unit running, something in the regeneration cycle isn't working correctly.

Is a water softener really necessary in Boulder?

It depends on your priorities. Boulder's water hardness is real, and a softener extends the life of water heaters and fixtures, but it's not an urgent problem the way a leak or gas smell is. We're happy to give you an honest read on whether it's worth it for your situation.

Can I install a water softener and a whole-home filtration system together?

Yes, and it's a common combination. The two systems serve different purposes, softening addresses hardness minerals, filtration addresses taste, sediment, or specific contaminants, so pairing them is straightforward when we plan for it upfront.
