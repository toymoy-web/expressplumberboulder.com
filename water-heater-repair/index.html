Why This Happens in Boulder.

Local Causes.

At roughly 5,430 feet, gas water heaters and their venting need to be sized and installed differently than a unit shipped for Denver or a coastal city. An undersized flue or a burner not adjusted for altitude shows up as pilot outages, weak hot water, or a unit that short-cycles.

Boulder's older homes, especially around Mapleton Hill and University Hill, sometimes still have water heaters vented into a shared masonry chimney built for a furnace decades ago. That setup can create backdrafting problems that a straight swap-and-go replacement won't fix. Venting and combustion air requirements for gas appliances fall under International Plumbing Code (IPC) and International Fuel Gas Code standards, which Colorado has adopted statewide, and Boulder County's own building code incorporates as well.

Know the Signs.

Common Warning Signs.

No hot water, or hot water that runs out much faster than it used to.

Rumbling, popping, or knocking sounds from the tank (usually sediment buildup).

Water around the base of the unit, even a small amount.

Pilot light that won't stay lit, or a gas unit that smells faintly of gas.

Rusty or discolored hot water from the tap.

How We Diagnose It.

Finding the Actual Problem.

We start by checking the thermocouple, gas valve, and venting before assuming the tank itself has failed. A surprising number of "dead" water heaters are actually a bad thermocouple or a tripped high-limit switch. For tankless units, we pull the error code from the unit's display and check the venting and water flow sensor before opening anything up.

If the tank itself is leaking from the bottom, that's almost always the tank shell corroding from the inside, and no repair fixes that permanently. We'll tell you honestly when repair versus replacement is the right call instead of billing for a fix that won't hold. Boulder homes with a water heater installed before roughly 2012 are the ones we see this in most often.

Common Questions.

Straight Answers.

Do you repair tankless water heaters, not just tank units?

Yes. Tankless units usually fail with an error code rather than a total shutdown, and most of the time it's a venting, water flow, or descaling issue rather than the unit itself.

Is it cheaper to repair or replace an old water heater?

It depends on the unit's age and what's actually wrong. A tank past 10 to 12 years old that's leaking from the shell is a replacement, not a repair. A younger unit with a failed part is usually worth fixing. We'll give you both numbers before you decide.
