Why This Happens in Boulder.

The Boulder Factor.

This is a especially familiar pattern in South Boulder, where hillside runoff near Viele Lake puts real seasonal pressure on basements. Boulder's spring snowmelt runoff period is when we see the sharpest spike in sump pump calls, particularly in homes with finished basements where even a small amount of water causes real damage. A pump that's worked fine for years can still fail right when it's needed most if it hasn't been tested recently.

Homes closer to Boulder Creek and the lower-lying parts of the city tend to see higher water tables during peak runoff, which puts more sustained demand on a sump pump than homes on higher ground see in the same storm.

Know the Signs.

What to Look For.

The pump not turning on during active water intrusion.

A pump running constantly without lowering the water level.

Unusual grinding or rattling noise from the pump.

Water in the basement despite having a sump pump installed.

The pit filling faster than the pump can keep up.

A pump that hasn't been tested in over a year.

What's Actually Going On.

The Pump Isn't Turning On When It Should.

A sump pump that stays silent during a rain event, or one that runs constantly without actually lowering the water level, both point to the same underlying question: is the pump itself dead, or is something else in the system failing. We check the float switch, check valve, and discharge line before assuming the pump motor is the problem.

How We Diagnose It.

Getting to the Root Cause.

We test the float switch, check valve, and discharge line, since a stuck float or a failed check valve can look identical to a dead pump from the homeowner's side. If the pump motor itself has failed, we'll confirm that before recommending replacement.

We also check where the discharge line actually terminates outside the house. A line that dumps too close to the foundation can recycle the same water right back toward the sump pit, making a working pump look like it's failing to keep up.

Installations follow standard discharge and check-valve placement guidelines rather than whatever was easiest to route, since a poorly placed discharge line is a common reason a technically-working pump still seems to underperform.

Common Questions.

What Homeowners Ask.

Should I get a battery backup for my sump pump?

If your basement is finished or holds anything you'd hate to lose, it's worth serious consideration. Power outages during storms are exactly when a sump pump is needed most, and a battery backup keeps it running when the power doesn't.

How often should a sump pump be tested?

A quick test, pouring water into the pit to confirm the float triggers the pump, takes a couple of minutes and is worth doing before the spring runoff season each year.

Can a sump pump handle a full basement flood on its own?

A properly sized pump handles normal groundwater intrusion well, but a genuinely major flood event can exceed any single pump's capacity. A backup pump on a separate circuit adds real protection for the worst-case scenario, not just for power outages.
