Backflow Testing & Prevention in Boulder, CO

Backflow prevention keeps contaminated water from siphoning back into the clean water supply, and it's typically a code and utility requirement for irrigation systems and many commercial connections in Boulder. Boulder Express Plumbing tests, certifies, and repairs backflow prevention devices.

Why This Happens in Boulder.

Why It Happens Here.

This requirement comes up constantly in irrigation-heavy areas like Southeast Boulder. Boulder's irrigation-heavy landscaping, from residential sprinkler systems to larger commercial and HOA installations, means backflow preventer testing is a routine annual requirement for a lot of properties rather than an occasional concern. The local water utility typically requires documented annual testing for these connections.

New irrigation installations, whether residential or commercial, also generally require a backflow preventer to be installed and certified before the connection is approved, which is worth factoring into any landscaping project timeline.

Know the Signs.

Signs to Watch For.

An annual testing notice from your water utility.

A new irrigation system installation requiring a backflow preventer.

A commercial property connection requiring certified testing.

A visible leak or failure at an existing backflow device.

Preparing for a property sale where backflow certification is needed.

A fire suppression system connection requiring its own certified device.

What's Actually Going On.

I Got a Notice From the Water Utility and Don't Know What It Means.

A lot of backflow calls start with a notice from the water utility rather than any visible plumbing problem at all. If you have an irrigation system, a fire suppression connection, or certain commercial fixtures, your property likely has a backflow preventer that requires certified annual testing, and the utility notice is simply their reminder that it's due.

The test itself is quick and non-invasive. We're not looking for a reason to sell you anything, we're confirming the device still does its job and getting you the documentation your utility actually asked for.

How We Diagnose It.

How We Track Down the Cause.

Testing involves checking the device under pressure to confirm it prevents reverse flow the way it's designed to. If a device fails testing, we can typically repair or replace it on the same visit and provide the certification documentation your utility requires.

We file the required documentation with the appropriate utility or authority ourselves in most cases, so you're not stuck figuring out where the paperwork needs to go after the test is done.

Before we test a fire suppression connection specifically, we confirm the system is properly isolated so testing doesn't interfere with active fire protection coverage during the visit. Devices and testing procedures themselves follow recognized cross-connection control standards, not an informal check.

Common Questions.

Questions We Get Often.

Do I really need annual backflow testing?

If your property has an irrigation system or certain commercial connections, most Boulder-area water utilities require it annually, and failing to test can result in a utility notice or service issue. It's worth confirming your specific requirement with your utility if you're unsure.

What happens if my backflow preventer fails testing?

We can usually repair or replace it the same day and re-certify it, so you're not left without documentation for your utility.

Who actually needs backflow testing, just commercial properties?

No, plenty of residential properties need it too, mainly homes with irrigation systems or certain fire suppression connections. If you're not sure whether your property has a backflow preventer at all, we can check that for you.

How long does a backflow test take?

Most single-device tests take well under an hour, and we can usually schedule around your availability rather than requiring a specific time window.
